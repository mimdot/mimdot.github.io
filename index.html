Mohammad Reza Nasirzadeh

Astrophysicist  ·  IPM School of Astronomy, Tehran, Iran

I am an astrophysicist working on magnetic fields in the interstellar medium. My research uses radio polarimetry and Faraday tomography to trace magnetic structures in molecular clouds, focusing on the Perseus molecular cloud and the GMIMS survey. I also study radio continuum emission and spectral energy distribution fitting of HII regions in M33 (the Triangulum Galaxy), under the guidance of Dr. Mehrnoosh Tahani (University of South Carolina). My broader interests span multi-wavelength radio astronomy, cosmic ray propagation, and large-survey statistical methods.

Science

Research

My work sits at the intersection of radio polarimetry, ISM physics, and multi-wavelength data analysis. I am particularly drawn to questions about how magnetic fields shape the environments where stars form, and how synchrotron and free-free processes encode the thermal and non-thermal history of nearby galaxies.

  • Magnetic fields in molecular clouds and the diffuse ISM
  • Faraday rotation and Faraday tomography (GMIMS survey)
  • Radio continuum emission & SED fitting for HII regions in M33
  • Radio–infrared correlation and cosmic ray propagation
  • Multi-tracer correlation methods (H-alpha, Planck dust, Fermi-LAT)
  • Star formation in galactic and extra-galactic environments
  • Large-survey statistical and spectral analysis

Current project

Exploring magnetic fields in molecular clouds through Faraday rotation and Faraday tomography, in collaboration with the GMIMS (Global Magneto-Ionic Medium Survey) international team. Supervised by Dr. Mehrnoosh Tahani, University of South Carolina.
